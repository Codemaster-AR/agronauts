### VPD Scans
VPD stands for Vapor Pressure Deficit.
For saplings, clones, and newly rooted cuttings, a lower Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD) is better, specifically in the range of 0.4 to 0.8 kPa. 
If the VPD is too high (>1.0 kPa) for a young plant, the rapid water loss causes excessive stress, wilting, and potential leaf burn.
If the VPD is too close to zero (<0.4 kPa), the plant cannot transpire enough to move nutrients, leading to stunted growth. 
To lower the Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)—which means making the air less dry and more humid—you need to increase the Relative Humidity (RH) or decrease the temperature in your growing space. A lower VPD (typically 0.4–0.8 kPa) is ideal for clones, seedlings, and early vegetative growth. Example: Turn on the fan less often and at a little higher temperatures. Do not turn off the fan for long, so that VPD is not too low. Alternatively:
Use a Humidifier: Position an ultrasonic humidifier inside your grow tent, preferably near the air intake.
Other insights:
2.8 kPa vapor pressure deficit (VPD) is very high and considered bad for a sapling. 
For young plants, clones, and seedlings, the optimal range is typically between 0.4 kPa and 0.8 kPa. A 2.8 kPa environment is extremely dry, causing excessive water loss, stress, and potential death for young, vulnerable plants. 
Here is a breakdown of why this is dangerous:
Excessive Transpiration: At 2.8 kPa, the air is so dry that the sapling will lose water faster than it can replace it from its small root system.
Stomatal Closure: To prevent drying out, the plant will close its stomata (pores). This stops photosynthesis, and the plant stops growing.
Nutrient Lockout: When VPD is too high, the plant cannot take up nutrients properly, leading to deficiencies, even if nutrients are present in the soil.
Symptoms: You will likely see leaf curling, cupping, and crispy leaf edges. 
How to Fix It:
To bring the VPD down to a safer range for a sapling, you must increase humidity (using a humidifier or humidity dome) or lower the temperature. For reference, the ideal VPD for vegetative growth is 0.8–1.2 kPa, and it should only be in the high range (>1.6 kPa) during the late stages of flowering.
